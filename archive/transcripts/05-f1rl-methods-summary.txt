What Yosh actually did

People watch these videos and think:

"Wow, RL discovered amazing driving."

But that's not really the story.

The real story is:

RL got stuck.

Then Yosh changed observations, rewards, curricula, training setup, or search procedure.

Then RL started working again.

That pattern repeats in every video.

Method 1: Curriculum spawning everywhere

This is probably the most relevant thing for your project.

On the long Trackmania map he says:

The AI regularly spawns anywhere on the map during training. This prevents it from focusing too much on the first turns.

This is a huge deal.

Your Monza PPO spent months repeatedly failing in the first kilometer.

Yosh's response would be:

Don't force the AI to replay the first kilometer every episode.

Instead:

spawn at 0m
spawn at 500m
spawn at 1000m
spawn at 2000m
spawn at 3000m

and learn the whole track simultaneously.

That is exactly how he got progress on long maps.

Method 2: Add observations whenever failure reveals missing information

Yosh repeatedly discovers:

AI fails →
AI probably can't see something →
add observation.

Examples:

Track shape

He added:

future corner information
upcoming path geometry

when the AI needed anticipation.

Car orientation

He added:

full car orientation
wheel contact states
wheel sliding states

because the AI was exploiting road-edge physics.

This is extremely important for Monza.

The question I'd ask:

Does your PPO actually know enough about the upcoming track?

Because most of your failures look like:

full throttle
late realization
panic steering

which often means:

insufficient anticipation.

Method 3: Temporary reward shaping

This is one of the smartest things he does.

Example:

He wants drifting.

He adds drift reward.

AI exploits it.

He fixes exploit.

AI learns drift.

Then:

he removes the drift reward

Once the skill exists.

Continue training without the bonus. Now that it discovered how to drift, the AI shouldn't forget it.

This is subtle.

He doesn't permanently bake drift reward into the objective.

He uses it as a scaffold.

Your Monza project has mostly used permanent shaping.

Yosh's approach is:

induce skill
remove scaffold
optimize actual objective again
Method 4: Force exploration by changing the environment

This is everywhere.

A06 example:

AI won't use ramp cuts.

So he adds walls.

The safe line becomes impossible.

Now AI must discover ramp cuts.

This is huge.

Your PPO repeatedly discovers:

full throttle until crash

What would Yosh do?

Probably not:

add another tiny penalty

Instead:

construct training situations where full throttle fails immediately.

Method 5: Reference-state curriculum

This is probably the most important thing you are not doing enough.

A06:

He saves flip positions.

Then repeatedly spawns training from them.

During training, the AI will regularly practice driving from one of these flip positions.

That is almost exactly what your focused-window curriculum became.

But Yosh uses:

successful states

not merely

arbitrary distance windows

That's a crucial distinction.

Method 6: Reward engineering around risk

The noseboost video is incredibly relevant.

He discovers:

AI refuses the high-risk strategy.

Why?

Because average reward is safer.

So he changes reward scale:

low-risk behavior becomes mediocre
high-risk success becomes hugely valuable

Immediately behavior changes.

This sounds very similar to your Monza situation.

Your PPO may be optimizing:

survive and collect easy progress

instead of

discover aggressive braking/corner-entry strategies.

Method 7: Explicit exploit hunting

He constantly watches telemetry.

Examples:

drifting exploit
noseboost exploit
upside-down sliding exploit
reward farming exploit

Every time:

observe weird behavior
understand it
redesign objective

He treats exploit discovery as normal.

Method 8: Multi-stage objectives

This is extremely important.

For noseboost:

Stage 1:

reward speed

Stage 2:

learn noseboost

Stage 3:

reward navigation

Stage 4:

reward track completion

He doesn't try to solve everything simultaneously.

Your Monza project is often trying to solve:

launch
racing line
braking
cornering
lap completion

with one objective.

Yosh repeatedly decomposes.

Method 9: Segmented search

This is the closest thing to evolutionary search.

A01 TAS challenge:

He divides track into segments.

For each segment:

run many attempts
keep best
continue from best state

This is not standard PPO.

This is much closer to:

beam search
evolutionary selection
elite retention

than classical RL.

This is highly relevant.

Method 10: Bruteforce / evolutionary search

The biggest discovery in the world-record videos was not PPO.

It was:

search

He repeatedly uses:

thousands of variations
random perturbations
keep best outcome
continue from best

That is essentially evolutionary optimization.

What I would steal for Monza

In order of importance:

1. Continuous controls

You already started this.

Yosh repeatedly benefits from precise control.

2. Successful-state curriculum

Not:

train around 900m

Instead:

train from saved successful corner-entry states.

3. Multi-stage rewards

Train:

corner entry
braking

corner exit

independently.

4. Population search

Not on policy weights.

On:

reward coefficients
curriculum schedules
observation choices
5. Segment elite search
